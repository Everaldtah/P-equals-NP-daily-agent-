# 2026-11-25 L-meta-11794 (solid refute, 46 points)

## Statement
"It is false that: At fan-in two, one NOT gate saves nothing on edges 0-4 1-4 0-5 2-5 0-6 1-6 2-6 3-6 4-6 5-6."
Negation of L-meta-11793: there is a fan-in-2 AND/OR/NOT circuit with at most one NOT gate computing the
7-vertex graph function f(x) = OR over edges (u,v) of x_u x_v, such that NO monotone fan-in-2 circuit with at
most as many gates computes f. Desk sketch: "a proof is one explicit counterexample ...; none is expected if
that statement is true."

## What I tried
- Read the semantics in PEqualNP/Defs/CircuitLowerBounds.lean: GateOp.eval is clean (and = all, or = any,
  not = !headD false, missing wires read false). No quirk lets a NOT gate do something a monotone gate cannot
  (constants are available monotonically too: and [] = true, or [] = false).
- Hand upper bounds for the monotone circuit: f = x6(x0..x5) or x4(x0 or x1) or x5(x0 or x2); several
  arrangements, e.g. (x4 or x6)(x0 or x1) or (x5 or x6)(x0 or x2) or x6(x3 or x4 or x5), all give 11 gates.
- A refutation needs (1) an explicit one-NOT circuit and (2) a Lean proof that no monotone circuit of that
  size exists, i.e. an exhaustive lower bound over all gate lists with arbitrary wire indices, without
  native_decide. (2) is far out of reach; no SAT solver is available here to even explore it, and the desk
  itself expects no counterexample.
